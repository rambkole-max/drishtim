Drishtim · logo concepts, batch 3

Two per route

Eight marks across the four positioning routes. Same test as before: large, lockup, 40px, 22px favicon, ink and paper.

1 · Reach & attention

Says "we get you seen and chosen." Positions Drishtim as a marketing partner on retainer.

K

Feed grid

Nine cells, one lit. The grid is the thing every client already understands — and the lit cell is the post that worked.

Mono · on paper

Most immediately "social media" of anything so far. A restaurant owner gets it in half a second. Risk: grids of squares are a crowded space — the tonal step on the unlit cells is what keeps it from generic.

L

Ripple

One point, three expanding waves. Literally "built once, shipped everywhere" — your tagline as a diagram.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

The clearest business argument. One shoot, many channels, growing reach — that's the retainer pitch in a symbol. It also animates perfectly: waves emit outward on load. Risk: reads as wifi if the arcs are too thin.

2 · Craft & capture

Says "we make beautiful things." Truer to how you work today; caps you as a production studio.

M

Contact sheet

Six frames, one circled in grease pencil. The mark isn't the camera — it's the judgement about which frame is the good one.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

The only craft mark about taste rather than gear. "No templates, no stock, no shortcuts" is exactly this picture. Risk: the grease-pencil ellipse needs to stay hand-drawn in feel or it goes corporate.

N

Shutter slit

Two focal-plane curtains with a band of light between them. The moment of exposure, not the equipment.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

Boldest shape in every batch. Survives being stamped on anything, at any size, in one colour. Risk: abstract enough that without the wordmark it could be a logo for almost anything.

3 · Sight meets reach

Fuses the optical form with a signal form. Covers the whole offer — harder to resolve cleanly.

O

Broadcast lens

An aperture that transmits. The hexagon opening stays, but waves leave it — capture on the left, reach on the right.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

The most complete story. We shoot it, then we make sure people see it — both halves of the business in one mark. Risk: two ideas fighting for room; the arcs are the first thing to break at small sizes.

P

Play frame

A play triangle sitting in a frame, with the frame's right edge opening into a grid. Reels are the product; the grid is where they land.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

Names the actual product. You sell ten reels a month — this is the only mark in thirty that shows one. Risk: the play triangle is the single most-used symbol on the internet; the frame-plus-grid is doing all the differentiating work.

4 · Name-led, no metaphor

Positioning comes from the words and the site, not the symbol. Hardest to get wrong.

Q

Caret

The word, then a blinking cursor bar. Digital-native without a single camera reference — and it implies "still typing, still shipping."

On paper drishtim Mono avatar

Zero cliché risk. No lens, no eye, no grid — and the blink gives you free motion in every video end card. Risk: a caret is a tech/dev signal more than a creative one.

R

Extended M

The final stroke of the M keeps going and becomes a rule you can use everywhere — under headlines, along a page edge, across a reel end card.

On paper Mono avatar

A logo that becomes a layout system. The rule is the reusable asset — it can run under any headline on any deliverable and the brand still reads. Risk: without the rule it's just bold type.
