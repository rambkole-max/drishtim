Drishtim · logo concepts, batch 2

Six more ways to see

Wider than batch 1 — a type-led route, a filmstrip route, an eye, a rangefinder, a prism, and a pure typographic block. Same palette and same test: large, lockup, 40px, 22px favicon, ink and paper.

E

Tittle lens

No icon at all. The name has two i's — both dots become aperture openings. The logo is the word.

Wordmark · on ink drıshtım Small drıshtım Avatar / favicon — one tittle, cropped
On paper drıshtım Mono avatar

The most sophisticated route. Nothing to explain, nothing to bolt on — and the hexagon tittle becomes a standalone icon for the favicon and Instagram avatar. Risk: needs the wordmark to be present to work, so it's weaker when you only have a tiny square.

F

Film frame

A single 35mm frame with its sprocket holes. Directly the rail already running down the side of the site.

G

Eye

Two arcs, one hexagon pupil. The oldest symbol for sight, drawn geometrically rather than illustratively.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

Clearest meaning, highest cliché risk. Eye marks are everywhere in media and surveillance branding. The hexagon pupil is what rescues it — it says lens, not iris.

H

Split rangefinder

The split-prism focusing circle from a manual camera. Two halves misaligned — you turn the ring until they meet.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

The best story of the ten. "We're the ones who bring it into focus" is the whole studio positioning in one symbol — and the animation writes itself: the halves slide into alignment on load. Risk: only photographers recognise the reference unaided.

I

Prism

One beam in, three out. Your tagline is "built once, shipped everywhere" — this is that sentence as a shape.

Mark · on ink
Lockup
Drishtim
Mono · on paper

Says what you sell, not what you own. One shoot becoming reels, stills and ads is the actual product. It's also the only concept here that justifies using all three brand colours at once. Risk: the full ray version needs space — small sizes fall back to triangle-plus-hexagon, which loses the idea.

J

Type block

No symbol at all. The name set tight in two lines inside a rule — a publisher's colophon, not a startup logo.

Mark · on ink
Lockup — horizontal
Mono · on paper

Cheapest to own, hardest to get wrong. No mark to redraw, no icon to explain, and it stamps onto anything — a menu corner, a reel end card, an invoice. Risk: it's a wordmark, so it never becomes a symbol people recognise without reading.
